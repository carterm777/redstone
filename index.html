01

Separation & Divorce

Separation and divorce rarely follow one script. Some couples agree on almost everything and need a straightforward, uncontested path; others are working through real disagreement over parenting, property, or support that has to be negotiated, or, if it comes to that, argued in court. We help you understand what you're dealing with, what the one-year separation requirement (or a faster path where adultery or cruelty applies) means for your timeline, and what your options look like at each stage — so decisions get made based on your circumstances, not guesswork.

Learn more about separation and divorce
02

Parenting, Custody & Relocation

Parenting, custody, and relocation decisions carry more weight than almost anything else you'll face — how you and your child's other parent will share parenting time, decision-making, and day-to-day life going forward. We build parenting plans and schedules where cooperation is possible, represent you in high-conflict disputes where it isn't yet, and handle relocation and mobility matters — whether a move is within Alberta, to another province, or outside Canada — where a parent's plans raise real questions for the other parent's rights. Whatever your starting point, the plan is built around your family's situation, not a form.

Learn more about parenting, custody, and relocation
03

Family Property Division

Family property division covers more than the house. Real estate, bank accounts, RRSPs, TFSAs, pensions, CPP credits, business interests, and debts all factor into a fair split, and getting the valuation and disclosure right matters as much as the division itself. We help work out what belongs in the calculation and what doesn't, pursue unequal-division or exemption claims where they apply, and, where it's warranted, seek orders to protect property — possession, sale, or preservation — while the matter gets resolved. Complicated finances are exactly where a second, careful set of eyes earns its keep.

Learn more about property division
04

Child Support

Child support in Alberta follows the federal and provincial Guidelines, but the number on a table rarely tells the whole story. We work through the base calculation, the Section 7 add-ons for costs like childcare, medical, dental, and education, and the harder situations — a self-employed payor, imputed income, a retroactive claim, or an ex who has simply stopped paying. If support has already been ordered and isn't being followed, enforcement is part of what we handle too. The goal is a number that reflects your family's real finances, not just a guideline default.

Learn more about child support
05

Family Agreements

Family agreements only protect you if they're drafted properly and each side gets independent legal advice before signing — a shortcut here tends to cost far more later than it saves now. Whether it's a separation, cohabitation, prenuptial, or postnuptial agreement, we draft these from the ground up, or review and advise on one someone else has already drafted for you, at a flat rate you know before you start. It's the same principle behind every flat-rate service on this site: you should know what something costs before you agree to sign it, not after.

Learn more about family agreements
06

Protection & Emergency Matters

Protection and emergency matters can't wait for a scheduled consultation. If you need an Emergency Protection Order, are dealing with family violence, or need an urgent parenting application heard quickly, that urgency is the whole point — and we treat it that way. Emergency matters get same-day contact with a lawyer, subject to availability. We help with the application itself, what to expect at the hearing, and what a protection order does and doesn't cover once it's in place. If you're not sure whether your situation counts as an emergency, call anyway — that's a question for us to answer, not you.

08First Conversations

A Few Real Questions

These come up in almost every first conversation we have. If yours isn't here, that's still a good reason to call.

01 / 05

In most cases, Alberta requires spouses to live separately for one year before a divorce is granted, though a shorter path exists where adultery or cruelty can be shown. That mark is usually where the process starts, not where it ends — the rest depends on whether parenting, support, and property get agreed on or need to be resolved through negotiation or the courts.

Alberta courts decide parenting arrangements based on what's in the child's best interests, not on which parent asks first. Sole decision-making responsibility is possible in the right circumstances, but it's the exception rather than the starting point — most arrangements involve some form of shared responsibility. What's realistic in your situation is exactly what a first conversation is for.

Don't wait. Being served means real deadlines are already running, so read everything you were given, keep every document, and find out what response is required and by when before you do anything else. Missing a response deadline can hurt your case regardless of how strong your position is.

Alberta courts generally expect separating families to attempt some form of resolution — direct negotiation, mediation, or a formal dispute-resolution conference — before a matter goes to a full hearing. What's required depends on your specific matter, but going in ready to negotiate first, while still prepared to go to court if needed, is usually the right approach.

Not without your consent or a court's approval, in most cases. Relocating with a child — within Alberta, to another province, or outside Canada — involves real legal requirements a parent has to meet first, and there are real options if you're worried it's about to happen without them. The sooner you get advice, the more options you usually have.
